# Evidence, Not Persuasion, Moves AI Shoppers

AI shopping agents decide in two stages, and the evidence now lets us say which levers work at each. In the first stage ("memory proposes"), the model's parametric familiarity with a brand sets the default consideration set: with identical specifications a recognized brand beat nine spec-identical fictional rivals in **100% of 670 trials** across GPT-4o-mini, Claude Sonnet and Gemini 3 Flash ([Chu & Hou, arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)). In the second stage ("retrieval disposes"), any legible delta that reaches the model overrides that default: a fictional brand wins half the time with just **+0.075 stars, a 7.3% discount, or 1.6× the reviews**, and a single authority sentence breaks the incumbent's monopoly in 73% of runs. The classic human toolkit transfers unevenly. Social proof, authority cues, platform badges, star ratings, review counts and top-row placement move agents far more than they move humans (effects of 10–60 percentage points versus ~10 for humans), while scarcity, urgency and exclusivity are null or backfire, decoys barely register, charm prices do nothing, and framing flips direction between models. The "third-party wall" is real but mis-described: it is a corpus-presence requirement on unbranded discovery queries, not a Wirecutter veto. One Wirecutter screenshot did shift a frontier agent's pick by **+99 points** ([Wharton GAIL, Aug 2026](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/technical-report-agentic-shopping/)), yet Wirecutter appears in only ~10% of "best product" answers (vendor data), official brand sites still supply **46.4%** of ChatGPT citations even on comparison queries ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)), and only 15.2% of ChatGPT product categories have a settled "owner" ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)). The wall is porous in niche categories, on branded and factual queries, and through Google Shopping placement and genuine review volume; it is nearly solid for mass-market electronics discovery. A company without mentions should build measurable corpus presence (retailer listings, authentic reviews, structured factual product data, real third-party coverage) and should not buy reviews, seed Reddit, inject hidden text or lean on GEO tone tricks, which now carry FTC, Google and Reddit enforcement risk and, on 2025–26 models, often reduce visibility.

A note on labeling: this report marks statistics produced by companies that sell AI-visibility, SEO, PR or listings tooling as **[vendor]**. Peer-reviewed or preprint academic studies and platform statements are labeled where relevant. Several 2026 preprints name models exactly as their authors did (e.g., "Claude Opus 4.8", "GPT-5.5", "claude-sonnet-4-6"); those designations are reported as given and were not independently verified.

## Memory proposes, retrieval disposes: a six-stage pipeline

No vendor publishes a ranking function. OpenAI, Perplexity, Google, Amazon and Microsoft all describe the same two-layer architecture, in which an LLM interprets the query and synthesizes an answer while a structured product layer (merchant feeds, Shopify Catalog, Google's Shopping Graph, Amazon's catalog) supplies candidates, prices and merchants. Every official statement is a list of factors without weights, and OpenAI's own feed spec says eligibility "does not guarantee display" ([OpenAI Product Feed Spec](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/specs/feed)). The most revealing official admission is OpenAI's April 2025 statement that product selection considers "ChatGPT's earlier model responses, before new search results" alongside third-party metadata and reviews ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/openai-introduces-shopping-to-chatgpt-with-1-billion-weekly-searches/)), which is a direct acknowledgement that parametric knowledge is an explicit input. Combining those partial descriptions with the independent measurements yields the reconstruction below. Three stages are documented by vendors; three are characterized only by outside measurement.

A workable formal model treats the probability that a given product is recommended as a product of stage-conditional probabilities: P(recommend) = P(reaches the model) × P(survives source trust | reached) × P(in shortlist | trusted) × P(final pick | shortlisted). The decomposition matters because the levers differ at each link and because failure is overwhelmingly concentrated at the first one. In a cross-provider audit of 7,763 cases where neither ChatGPT nor Claude recommended a brand, both diagnosed the same failure mode 95.1% of the time, and the dominant mode was discoverability (the brand never reached the model) rather than compellingness (reached, not mentioned) or positioning (mentioned, not recommended); agreement rose from 81% for category leaders to **99.6% for long-tail regional brands** ([Jack et al., arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)).

### Parametric memory supplies the default; retrieval overrides it only with a legible delta

The memory-versus-retrieval split is engine-dependent and poorly measured, and any single "X% from training data" figure should be distrusted. The one cross-provider estimate puts Claude recommending "from priors" **43–52%** of the time versus **8–29%** for ChatGPT ([arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)). Estimates of how often ChatGPT searches the web at all range from **14.4%** of responses in Ahrefs' 66.6 million logged-out GPT-5.6 responses **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)) through 23% (Texta), 31% (Nectiv) and 58% (Graphite) to 80.5% (Cloro), all from GEO vendors with incompatible methods ([Texta](https://www.texta.ai/blog/how-often-does-chatgpt-trigger-web-search-complete-analysis); [Graphite](https://graphite.io/five-percent/research/do-not-force-search-prompt-tracking); [Cloro](https://cloro.dev/blog/chatgpt_grounding_frequency_study/)). What is clear is that when retrieval does not fire, the brand must already live in the weights. The zero-shot ranking literature established early that LLM rankers favor popular items in proportion to their frequency ([Hou et al., ECIR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.08845v2)), and the Ahrefs correlation data are consistent with this: across 75,000 brands, branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at **Spearman 0.664**, against 0.326 for Domain Rating and 0.218 for backlinks **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)).

The Incumbent Advantage experiments give the cleanest picture of how retrieval overrides memory. At identical specifications the real brand wins 100% of the time (Incumbent Advantage Index 10.0, the theoretical maximum). With clearly better fictional specs the real brand falls to **1.7–4.6%**. The crossover is tiny: a fictional brand wins 50% of the time with a **+0.075-star** edge, a 7.3% discount or 1.6× the review count. Product parameters explained 82.4% of ranking variance, list position 6.5%, and brand identity only 1.2% ([arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)). The same hypersensitivity shows up in the Columbia/Yale ACES sandbox, where a +0.1 rating lifts a 10%-baseline product to 15.4% (Claude Sonnet 4), 20.3% (GPT-4.1) or 16.0% (Gemini 2.5 Flash), and doubling the review count buys price headroom of 17–37% ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)). The practical reading is that brand familiarity is a tiebreaker, not a moat, but a challenger's advantage has to be structured and visible to the model to count.

### Source trust is platform-specific, volatile and only loosely tied to authority

The retrieval layer behaves less like an editorial filter and more like a sampling process with platform-specific priors. In mid-2025 Wikipedia was ChatGPT's single largest source at **7.8% of all citations** and Reddit was Perplexity's at 6.6% **[vendor]** ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)); by September 2025 ChatGPT's Reddit citations had collapsed from roughly 60% of responses to about 10% within six weeks **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/)), and by late September 2026 Reddit appeared in about 1 in 43 ChatGPT answers (down 88% from March) while PubMed rose from 64th to 4th most-cited domain and hands-on testers like RTINGS gained **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)). Google's two AI surfaces gave answers with 86% semantic similarity to the same queries but cited the same URLs only ~13% of the time **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-vs-ai-mode)), and ChatGPT and Gemini shared only **5.4%** of cited domains for the same commercial query, with zero overlap in 76.7% of comparisons ([Uberti-Bona Marin et al., arXiv 2609.18729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18729)). Authority is a weak gate: 47.9% of 20.5 million citations went to domains with DA under 40 **[vendor]** ([Wellows](https://wellows.com/research/does-domain-authority-matter-for-ai-citations/)), programmatic spam sites took more than 14% of ChatGPT citations for B2B "best" prompts **[vendor]** ([Overthink Group](https://overthinkgroup.com/b2b-ai-citation-stats-2026-q2/)), and cited Reddit posts had a median of 5–8 upvotes with semantic similarity to the answer, not engagement, predicting inclusion **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/reddit-ai-search-visibility-study/)).

Two structural facts about this layer matter for the model. First, licensing buys retrieval prominence on ChatGPT specifically: OpenAI-licensed publishers earned 10.2 citations per cited page versus 6.9 for unlicensed (+48%), OpenAI-exclusive publishers +112%, while Google-licensed publishers showed no AI Overviews premium and Perplexity licensees were at parity **[vendor]** ([Otterly/Press Ranger](https://insights.otterly.ai/ai-news-content-licensing-deals/)). Second, citation is not recommendation: **61.7%** of 3,981 cited domain appearances were "ghost citations" in which the brand was never named, and only 13.2% were both cited and mentioned **[vendor]** ([Semrush/Indig](https://semrush.com/blog/the-ghost-citations-study/)). In a 1,094-category study, only 21% of the most-cited domains were also the most-mentioned brand, with a slight negative correlation (−0.229) between the two **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)). Any model of agent choice therefore has to track citations and brand mentions as separate quantities.

### Run-to-run variance is large at the list level and small at the head of the set

The shortlist stage is stochastic, and the measurements now agree on its shape. In 2,961 volunteer runs of 12 brand-recommendation prompts across ChatGPT, Claude and Google AI, the chance of the same brand list on two runs was **under 1 in 100** and the same order under 1 in 1,000; yet in 994 headphone answers Bose, Sony, Sennheiser and Apple each appeared in **55–77%** of responses, tight categories (cloud providers) were stable, and broad ones (sci-fi novels) scattered ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)). Repeated-query studies show the brand set converging: ten calls recovered **92.6–95.2%** of the fifteen-call brand set across 4,500 responses ([Żatuchin, arXiv 2609.05059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05059)), and a generalizability analysis of ~190,000 observations found five runs reach a coefficient of 0.96 across 250 questions even though a single Gemini question scored 0.27 ([arXiv 2609.04047](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04047)). Daily tracking of 1,300+ commercial prompts over 28 days found only a quarter of ChatGPT's cited pages reappeared the next day, but the most frequently named brand appeared on **82%** of days (89% for AI Overviews) **[vendor]** ([Detailed](https://detailed.com/ai-volatility/)). Cross-model disagreement exceeds within-model noise: ChatGPT and Claude recommendation sets overlapped with Jaccard 0.35 against a same-prompt rerun baseline of 0.50–0.61 ([arXiv 2606.26116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26116)), API outputs overlapped only 12–15% with the same vendor's consumer interface ([arXiv 2609.18729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18729)), and model updates act as demand shocks: the Fitbit Inspire was chosen 45% of the time by Claude Sonnet 4, 77% by Claude Opus 4.5 and 6% by GPT-5.1 from the same assortment ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)). The modeling consequence is that rank within a single answer is noise, inclusion rate over 5–15 runs per engine is signal, and each engine must be modeled separately.

### The final pick is shaped by presentation and satisficing

Once a shortlist exists, the final choice is governed by platform-controlled presentation. All tested models over-select the top row of a results grid, but the favored column differs by model (GPT-4.1 first column, Claude middle columns, Gemini columns 3–4); for Claude Sonnet 4, moving a product from bottom-right (4.5% selection) to the top row raised selection about **fivefold**, a move worth roughly a doubling of price, and the effect persisted under "ignore position" instructions and in headless JSON interfaces; GPT-4.1 and GPT-5.1 showed nearly opposite position preferences ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)). An "Overall Pick" badge raised a 10% baseline to 24.3%, 19.9% and 42.6% across the three models, while a "Sponsored" tag lowered it to 8.9%, 8.0% and 7.9% ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)). In Microsoft's Magentic Marketplace simulation, customer agents accepted the first proposal received **80–100%** of the time (Claude Sonnet 4.5 93.3%, GPT-4o 100%), and consumer welfare fell for nearly every model as the number of visible businesses rose from 3 to 100 (Claude Sonnet 4 from 1,800 to 600 against an optimum near 2,200) because agents explored too few options ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-simulation-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/)). Agents also concentrate: choices cluster on a few "modal" products with some brands never chosen, a "departure from human consumers, whose preferences are more dispersed" ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)). The one documented defense sits with the consumer rather than the seller: explicit user profiles "act as switches that override other cues" ([ABxLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)), and stating a precise criterion restored agent optimality from 0.29–0.48 to 0.83–0.88 under costly inspection ([Tool-Lab, arXiv 2609.28372](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28372v1)).

## Social proof and authority transfer; scarcity, decoys and charm prices do not

Two literatures have to be kept apart. Studies that prompt an LLM to simulate a human respondent (Horton's "homo silicus", HBS conjoint work) show that models can reproduce human bias patterns when asked to predict a person; studies that let the model choose on its own account show something different: cue hypersensitivity rather than Kahneman–Tversky bias. The agent-side regularities that replicate across labs are not the classic cognitive biases but outsized responses to ratings, price, social-proof and authority text, platform endorsements and position. The 30-bias benchmark that tested 20 models in 30,000 scenarios found evidence of every bias in some model, but status quo bias and the disposition effect were the only two with negative averages, and bias did not correlate with model size or capability ([Malberg et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15413)). The Wharton persuasion studies establish that Cialdini's principles exist in the weights: seven principles more than doubled GPT-4o-mini's compliance with objectionable requests (33.3% to 72.0%), and on Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5 mini and Gemini 3 Flash with reasoning enabled the lift shrank to **35.3% to 51.3%** but stayed positive in all 21 model-by-principle cells ([Wharton GAIL 2025](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/persuading-llms-initial-study/); [Wharton GAIL 2026, PNAS](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/persuading-llms-objectionable-requests/)). | Prompt injection in page text | Works, model-dependent, a policy violation | Redirected all payments for GPT-4o, GPT-OSS-20b, Qwen3-4b; Claude Sonnet 4 resisted; hidden text in reviews/metadata succeeded 41.7–68.2% in StakeBench; agents start following injections 16–86% but complete the goal 0–17% | [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-simulation-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/); [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4184455/prompt-injection-breaks-todays-ai-agents-study-warns.html); [WASP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18575) |
| Keyword stuffing | Backfires | −8.7% to −9.1% visibility | [GEO](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3) |

The generation trend is a split, not a general hardening. Numeric rationality improved sharply: failure to pick a 1%-cheaper identical item fell from over 63% (Claude Sonnet 3.5) to about 9% (GPT-4.1), and the latest models "almost never" miss price differences ([ACES](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02630v2)); reasoning-trained models are near expected-value maximizers on risky choice ([Mind the (DH) Gap](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15173)); and frontier agents resisted fake awards and fake reviews that fooled small open-weight models ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/magentic-marketplace-an-open-source-simulation-environment-for-studying-agentic-markets/)). But preference-ordering biases with no normative answer did not shrink: reasoning siblings were not less biased than non-reasoning ones (Δ = +0.031 toward more bias) ([Kraishan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10049)); o3 and o4-mini showed the largest rating effects and still 38–48 pp nudge effects with ratings matched ([ABxLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)); large reasoning models "show no improvement in robustness" to bandwagon cues on subjective tasks ([arXiv 2504.09946](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09946v1)); and a "focus only on product features" system prompt left social-proof effects of +19.75 to +23.38 points intact ([Bias Beware](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01349v2)). Because pretraining backbone, not fine-tuning, is the main determinant of a model's bias profile ([arXiv 2507.07186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07186)), and because non-RLHF Mistral Large showed the same social-proof effect as RLHF models, part of the susceptibility is baked in at pretraining and will not be removed by alignment alone. The practical prediction for a seller is that numeric tricks will keep failing against capable agents while textual evidence cues, platform-structural cues and complete structured attributes will keep working, and that the direction of position effects will keep flipping with each model release.

Two caveats temper the whole table. Every agent-side effect size comes from a sandbox (ACES, OneStopMarket, Tool-Lab, a simulated hotel page, ten-item catalogs) rather than a live retailer, most are preprints, and human baselines are small (ABxLab n = 30). And the concentration of agent choice means a cue that works produces winner-take-most outcomes rather than the marginal share shifts seen with humans, which is why small cue changes translate into 10–60 point swings; a seller should read those numbers as the ceiling for a cue that reaches an unprotected agent, not as a market forecast.

## The wall is a corpus-presence requirement, not a Wirecutter veto

The claim under test is that a single review from an authoritative third party outweighs everything a company publishes on its own site, and that lacking such mentions is insurmountable. The evidence supports the first half as a description of *discovery-stage* behavior on unbranded queries, and rejects the second half as stated.

The evidence for the wall is substantial and mostly consistent. On unbranded discovery prompts, brand-owned domains receive **2.24%** of citations versus 30.7% on branded prompts (a 13.7× gap), and 85% of unbranded responses cited no brand-owned source at all, across 57.2 million citations **[vendor]** ([AirOps/Foundation](https://foundationinc.co/lab/branded-unbranded-ai-citations)). For 2,000 "best product" questions across five engines, **70%** of 17,524 answers cited at least one affiliate review site (Perplexity 99.4%, AI Overviews 79.3%, ChatGPT 61%, Gemini 54.7%), led by Forbes 14.4%, Wirecutter 10.3%, Consumer Reports 7.6%, TechRadar 7.3%, CNET 7.2%, Tom's Guide 7.2% and RTINGS 7.0% **[vendor]** ([Suff Digital](https://www.suffdigital.com/resources/data-studies/ai-product-answers-affiliate-sites)). In 3,312 product-intent responses across consumer electronics, wearables and home categories, the top sources were YouTube 19%, Reddit 19%, RTINGS 16%, Forbes 9%, PCMag 8% and CNET 8%, and the authors wrote that "Brand-owned domains are conspicuously absent from the top of this list" **[vendor]** ([Cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-shopping-chatgpt-recommends/)). Listicles took 40.9% of commercial-intent citations versus 21.9% overall **[vendor]** ([Wix Studio](https://wix.com/studio/ai-search-lab/research/content-types-most-cited-by-llms)). The closest thing to a quantified absence penalty is observational: when neither the brand nor its domain appeared in the retrieval path, the brand was mentioned in **2.8%** (GPT) and 3.8% (Gemini) of answers; with an own-domain citation present, 49.0% and 58.4% ([Tannenbaum, arXiv 2609.23162](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23162)). And the controlled agent evidence shows how heavily a recognized reviewer weighs once retrieved: a single Wirecutter screenshot moved Gemini 3.5 Flash **+99 points** toward Wirecutter's pick, Wirecutter dominated Reddit and The Strategist for both Claude models, and a Reddit-plus-Strategist combination moved no model ([Wharton GAIL](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/technical-report-agentic-shopping/)); in ABxLab, the Wirecutter-pick cue was the single largest nudge across 17 models ([ABxLab](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25609v2)). Finally, ChatGPT's own fan-out queries for running shoes contained "best" 206 times and "review" 117 times but "official" only 65 times **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)), meaning the engine actively goes looking for third-party verdicts.

The evidence against an *insurmountable* wall is equally concrete, and it clusters into five openings.

**Branded and factual queries are not walled.** Official websites supplied **63.4%** of ChatGPT citations for factual questions (pricing, features) and still **46.4%** for comparison questions; brand `site:` searches made up 75.9% of ChatGPT's fan-out searches to the most-searched domains, with apple.com, ikea.com, nike.com and samsung.com at the top **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)). Local and listings-heavy queries drew 86% "brand-managed" citations **[vendor]** ([Yext](https://www.yext.com/about/news-media/ai-citations-release)). Gemini and Google AI Overviews are the most vendor-site-friendly engines, with roughly three-quarters of their top-100 cited domains being vendors against one-quarter for ChatGPT and Perplexity **[vendor]** ([Overthink Group](https://overthinkgroup.com/b2b-ai-citation-stats-2026-q2/)). Owned content is retrieved to verify details about brands already in the candidate set; it is not irrelevant, it is downstream.

**Niche categories have thin corpora and open slots.** Only **15.2%** of 1,094 ChatGPT categories had a clear owner, 53.7% were unsettled, and high-demand topics were *more* open (11.3% owned) than low-demand ones (19%) **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-topic-authority-study/)). Across 3,750 responses on 250 category queries, 8.0% of queries were "competitive vacuums" concentrated in narrow segments (luxury fashion online CVI 0.87, "Electronics in Nordics" 0.73), the three models agreed on the top brand in only 41.6% of queries, and brands absent from 33–38 of 50 queries still achieved total ownership on specific ones ([arXiv 2606.23057](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23057v1)). SparkToro's tight-market finding (LA Volvo dealers and cloud providers consistent, broad categories scattered) points the same way ([SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/)). Even in mass-market electronics the top product (Garmin Forerunner 265) appeared in only ~2% of responses, with a product HHI of 0.0005 **[vendor]** ([Cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-shopping-chatgpt-recommends/)), so the barrier there is not a single incumbent but the depth of the third-party corpus that assembles a wide, churning shortlist.

**Feeds and Google Shopping are a side door for product cards.** In ChatGPT, 87.3% of 812,190 product cards came from web crawl and 12.7% from direct merchant feeds **[vendor]** ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)), and the top ChatGPT product appeared in Google Shopping's first three results **75%** of the time across 100 prompts × 5 runs **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/chatgpt-searches-google-shopping/)). Google states there are "no additional requirements" beyond being indexed and snippet-eligible to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), and Perplexity's CEO said merchants in its free program get "increased chances of being a recommended product" because richer data helps judge relevance ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/11/18/perplexity-ai-ceo-shopping-product-recommendation-search-tools)). Feeds affect eligibility and data quality rather than shortlist rank, but a product carousel is a route to being shown that does not run through an editorial review.

**Review volume and rating on retail surfaces substitute for editorial authority.** Top-ranked ChatGPT product cards had a median of **787 reviews** versus 352 for bottom-ranked **[vendor]** ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)); Amazon Rufus essentially never recommends products below 4.0 stars (median 4.5) across 1,300+ recommended products **[vendor]** ([PPC Land/Amalytix](https://ppc.land/rufus-shows-5-products-not-50-what-brands-must-know-about-amazons-ai-filter/)); and the controlled experiments show 1.6× reviews or +0.075 stars is enough to beat a familiar brand ([arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)). Retailer pages are themselves third-party sources: Best Buy appeared in 12% of electronics responses and Walmart in 11% **[vendor]** ([Cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/ai-shopping-chatgpt-recommends/)).

**The wall itself is unstable and low-fidelity.** Reddit's ChatGPT citation share swung from ~60% of responses to ~10% in six weeks **[vendor]** ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/)) and then fell 88% over 2026 as primary sources, brand support pages and hands-on testers rose **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/query-fan-out-chatgpt)). An obscure site (Toxigon) sat in ChatGPT's all-time top 10 cited domains in 2025 **[vendor]** ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns)), and ~16% of sources cited across four engines showed signs of being AI-generated ([Allaham & Diakopoulos, AIES 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23684)). Licensing does not even guarantee that a publisher's verdicts are reported correctly: ChatGPT linked to WIRED's buying guides while naming picks that were not in them ([TechBuzz summary](https://techbuzz.ai/articles/chatgpt-fails-product-recommendation-test-hallucinates-wired-picks)). A gate that admits programmatic spam and misquotes its own licensed partners is not a quality filter in the editorial sense; it is a retrieval-relevance filter that a well-structured, genuinely relevant page can pass.

On the specific Wirecutter formulation, the verdict is therefore split by stage. In the final-pick stage, with the review already in context, one recognized editorial endorsement does outweigh on-page attributes for most 2026 agents, and that is the strongest single seller-external lever measured. But no dataset shows Wirecutter as a gatekeeper of the candidate set: it is one of seven publishers in the 7–14% band on "best product" answers (behind Forbes), RTINGS rather than Wirecutter dominates the electronics data, Wirecutter's parent is litigating against OpenAI while Condé Nast, Dotdash Meredith and Axel Springer titles plausibly enjoy a ChatGPT licensing premium, and the Ahrefs correlation ordering (branded web mentions 0.664 > branded search volume 0.392 > DR 0.326) describes breadth of presence rather than any single endorsement **[vendor]** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). Two small-retailer findings bound the realistic outcome for a company that gets in without marquee coverage: small retailers were cited about as often as chains but chains received ~2.5× the leading recommendations and small stores got about 1 in 10 first-place slots on specific queries (Lightspeed-sponsored) ([Fit Small Business](https://fitsmallbusiness.com/smb-news/ai-shopping-recommendations-small-retailers/)), and a practitioner's three-month, 50-query tracking of a small B2B client logged 4 mentions against 47 for the market leader ([Roberto Serra](https://www.roberto-serra.com/en/geo/measuring-ai-visibility/tools/ai-visibility-tracking-tool-i-tested-peec-otterly-profound-3-months/)). Where the wall is near-solid is a specific intersection: unbranded discovery queries, in deep mass-market categories with heavy RTINGS/Wirecutter/Reddit/YouTube coverage, on Perplexity and ChatGPT. Everywhere else it is porous.

## What an unmentioned company should and should not do

The actionable evidence divides cleanly into measured levers, vendor-asserted levers with no controlled support, and tactics that are measured to fail or carry enforcement risk. No named small or DTC brand with before/after AI-visibility data tied to a specific intervention exists in the public record, despite strong commercial incentive for a dozen tooling vendors to publish one; that absence is itself a finding, and it means time-to-effect is unknown while month-to-month tracking swings of 40–60% would swamp most small interventions.

Three enforcement regimes now bite on the grey tactics. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (final August 2024) bans fake or AI-generated reviews, sentiment-conditioned compensation, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites and review suppression, with civil penalties for knowing violators ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)); warning letters went to ten companies in December 2025 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-warning-letters-ai-consumer-reviews)). Google's May 15, 2026 changelog made explicit that all spam policies, including cloaking for AI crawlers, scaled AI content and inauthentic brand mentions, apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-spam-policies-now-officially-cover-ai-overviews-and-ai-mode-in-search)). Reddit is detecting and removing coordinated inauthentic accounts and has publicly questioned "the legitimacy of any provider suggesting they can" guarantee LLM pickup ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/daily-news-roundup/thursday-04062026/)). The asymmetry is stark: the measured upside of these tactics is unproven and decaying as frontier models learn to resist them, while the downside is codified. The Incumbent Advantage paper adds a game-theoretic warning for even the legitimate text levers: when all nine fictional brands adopted the same authority language, the individual payoff fell from +0.802 to +0.007, the incumbent recovered to 93.8%, and non-adopters received zero recommendations across 4,745 trials ([arXiv 2606.17443](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17443v1)), which C-SEO Bench independently describes as a "congested, zero-sum game" ([C-SEO Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11097v3)). Copy-level optimization is hygiene that keeps a brand from being dropped; it is not a durable advantage.

The stakes of getting in are rising, which is the honest case for investing in corpus presence rather than tricks. AI-referred traffic to US retailers grew 393% year over year in Q1 2026 and converted 42% more often than non-AI traffic (reversing a 38% deficit a year earlier) **[vendor/secondary]** ([Digital Commerce 360 on Adobe](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/04/23/ecommerce-trends-ai-key-conversion-metric/)), and after OpenAI made brand links more prominent in May 2026 the share of ChatGPT referrals landing on brand homepages jumped from ~26–32% to ~60% **[vendor/secondary]** ([Similarweb summaries](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/geo/gen-ai-stats/)). Publishers are being read far more than visited (Anthropic's crawl-to-referral ratio was ~38,000:1 and OpenAI's ~1,100:1 in mid-2025, from secondary compilations of Cloudflare data) ([SEOmator](https://seomator.com/blog/crawl-to-refer-ratio-ai-crawlers-llm-bots)), and RTINGS paywalled its full test data in March 2026 citing uncredited AI scraping ([Wikipedia: RTINGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTINGS)). A brand that earns a stable slot in the shortlist head captures above-average downstream value while the third-party corpus that gates it is simultaneously thinning and shifting toward primary sources and hands-on testers.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing from this evidence is that AI agents are not persuadable consumers so much as credulous evidence-weighers with a short attention span. They do not fall for the decoy, the charm price or the countdown timer, and they increasingly see through fake awards and fake reviews; but they over-weight any legible, structured signal of quality they do see (a tenth of a star, a review count, a recognized reviewer's verdict, a platform badge), they satisfice on the first adequate option, and their defaults come from whatever the training corpus said about a category. That profile means the seller's problem is epistemic rather than rhetorical: the brand must exist, with verifiable attributes, in the places the model samples when it builds its candidate set, and the places it samples are drifting from Reddit and encyclopedias toward primary sources, official pages, retailer review systems and testers. The "mentions are the new backlinks" slogan is directionally right and causally unproven; the better operating hypothesis is "corpus presence is the new indexation."

Three implications follow for anyone modeling or measuring this. First, measure inclusion rate per engine over 5–15 runs and ignore rank, because list order is noise while the head of the set is stable and engine-specific. Second, treat each model release as a demand shock and each category as its own market: a brand's share can move from 62% to 5% on a version change with no action of its own, and 54% of categories have no settled owner to displace. Third, the window in which text-level cues produce large swings is closing from two directions at once: frontier models flag manipulative copy at 99–100% while competitors' adoption drives the payoff of legitimate cues toward zero. What remains after both forces play out is the unglamorous core of the pipeline: being retrievable, being accurately described, being reviewed by real people, and being in the sub-categories where the candidate pool is small enough that showing up is most of the battle.
